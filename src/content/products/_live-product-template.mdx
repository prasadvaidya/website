## How it works

Explain the product in three to five steps. Start with the user’s input, show the important product behavior, and end with the result. Link to a demo, documentation, or example when that helps someone evaluate the product.

1. Describe the first action a customer takes.
2. Explain what the product handles for them.
3. Show the useful output or completed outcome.

## Why this approach is different

Compare the product with the current workaround or familiar alternative. Focus on the tradeoff you chose and why it matters to this audience; avoid unsupported “best,” “fastest,” or “revolutionary” claims.

## Built for the real workflow

Add the details that remove purchase anxiety: integrations, data handling, reliability, onboarding, support, or a clear limitation. Use descriptive subheadings that contain the phrases a prospective customer would search for.

## The story behind the product

Briefly explain why you built it and what firsthand evidence shaped it. This is where the personal brand adds trust to the product without making the page about the founder.
