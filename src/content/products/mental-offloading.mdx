## Too many tabs stay open in working memory

Unfinished thinking has a mental state. For a complex task, it includes the goal, decisions, constraints, open questions, and next move. For a creative idea, it can be a mood, intention, fragment, reference, or possibility that has not yet become a finished work.

When a meeting, message, second project, or the end of the day pulls you away, that state has nowhere reliable to go. The result is mental clutter: you either keep rehearsing unfinished work in the background or accept the cost of reconstructing it later.

The product concept is designed to make that transition explicit: a handoff from you now to you later, whether you are switching projects or letting an idea develop over time.

## A handoff between work sessions

The first version is focused on a simple loop:

1. Before switching tasks or ending the day, offload a short checkpoint while the work is still fresh.
2. Capture where the thinking stands, what matters about it, the open threads, and the next promising direction.
3. When you return, use that handoff to re-enter the task without replaying the previous session from memory.

The goal is not to store everything you know. It is to preserve just enough working state to lower cognitive overload, close one mental loop, and fully enter the next.

## Built for the boundaries in a real day

This workflow should be useful whenever continuity breaks: moving from client work to product work, pausing deep work for a meeting, walking away from something half-finished to collect a child or run an errand, handing an unfinished problem to tomorrow morning, or preserving the spark behind a song or story until it is ready to grow.

A good offload should be faster than keeping the task in your head and more useful than a vague reminder that says “continue this.”

## What the first early-access group will shape

The concept is at the stage where the right users can still influence the product rather than merely react to it. Early access will focus on three questions:

- What is the fastest way to create a trustworthy mental handoff?
- Which pieces of task state make re-entry meaningfully easier?
- When and how should an offloaded task return to your attention?

If task switching leaves you mentally crowded—or resuming yesterday's work feels like detective work—your workflow is exactly the kind of evidence this early validation needs.

## Built from a problem I experience myself

I am exploring this product because switching between architecture work, product experiments, calls, and research creates the same friction in my own day. The hardest part is not remembering that a task exists; it is recovering the exact mental position from which useful work can continue.
