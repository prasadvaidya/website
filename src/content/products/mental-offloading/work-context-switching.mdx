## The hidden cost of a busy calendar is repeated reconstruction

Complex work depends on more than a task title. A developer may be holding the reason behind an architectural choice, an unresolved failure mode, and the next experiment. An executive may be balancing stakeholder positions, constraints, and a decision that is not ready to make.

When another responsibility interrupts, that working state does not transfer neatly. Part of it remains active as attention residue; the rest becomes lost context that has to be rebuilt when the task returns.

This workflow is designed to make the transition deliberate rather than accidental.

## A small handoff before every boundary

Before switching, it helps you create a compact checkpoint:

- What outcome am I currently trying to reach?
- Where exactly does the work stand?
- Which decisions and constraints matter?
- What remains unresolved?
- What is the next useful action?

The checkpoint is for your future self, not an activity report. It should preserve the working model that normally disappears between sessions.

## Useful across the shape of a real day

For a developer, moving between projects might mean leaving deep work on a feature for a production escalation and returning without rereading the entire issue. For an executive, it might mean moving from a hiring decision to a strategic plan to a customer problem while preserving the distinct logic of each.

The same boundary exists when you step out of work altogether. Leaving a half-finished piece of work to collect a child, run an errand, or handle something at home rarely feels like a professional transition, so nothing gets written down—and the work follows you through the chore instead, so you are fully present for neither.

The common need is continuity: the ability to give your attention to what is actually in front of you, because the unfinished project has a trustworthy place to wait.

## What early users will help validate

Early access will test how quickly a handoff can be created, which details most improve re-entry, and when an offloaded task should return to attention. The goal is not more documentation. It is less time and mental energy spent recovering work that was already underway.
