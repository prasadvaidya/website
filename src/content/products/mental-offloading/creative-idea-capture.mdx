## Capturing an idea is not the same as preserving it

A melody arrives with a certain motion. A sentence suggests a voice and a larger story. A visual fragment carries a mood that feels obvious in the moment.

Recording the fragment prevents it from disappearing completely, but the artifact is only part of the idea. When you return days or months later, the note may still exist while the reason it mattered has gone missing.

The product concept is designed to preserve that creative working state—not just the file or sentence it produced.

## A handoff from inspiration to development

When an idea cannot be developed immediately, a lightweight creative handoff can hold:

- The fragment itself
- The feeling or intention behind it
- References and associations it brought to mind
- Questions or possibilities still open
- A promising experiment for the next session

That is enough to let the idea rest without demanding that you finish it now.

## Return to the possibility, not an empty artifact

A songwriter should be able to hear why a voice memo felt worth keeping. An author should be able to recover the tension behind a scene fragment. A designer should be able to revisit the principle that made a rough concept coherent.

The workflow aims to shorten the distance between “I remember recording this” and “I know what I want to try next.”

## What early creators will help validate

Early access will explore which kinds of creative context are worth preserving, how capture can stay out of the way of inspiration, and how an older idea should be resurfaced when it is ready for attention.

The goal is not a larger idea archive. It is helping more promising fragments survive the journey into finished work.
