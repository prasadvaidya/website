## When you can't turn your brain off, work may still feel unfinished

The laptop closes, but working memory keeps holding tomorrow's priorities, a difficult conversation, the decision you did not make, and the task you are afraid you will forget.

That mental rehearsal can feel responsible, but it keeps too many tabs open. The workday has ended on the clock without ending in your attention.

This workflow is designed to create a reliable boundary between work and the rest of the day.

## A brain dump before bed that gives tomorrow a starting point

A useful offload does more than list everything on your mind. It turns mental clutter into a practical handoff:

1. Empty unfinished tasks, decisions, worries, and ideas while they are still active.
2. Add the small amount of context that explains why each thread matters.
3. Choose tomorrow's priority and the first action that will help you begin.

The result should help you clear your head tonight and pick up with less friction tomorrow.

## Build a shut down ritual you can trust

The best end of day shutdown is short enough to repeat and complete enough to feel trustworthy. It should not become another elaborate productivity system or demand that every open loop be resolved.

The workflow aims to make the ritual consistent: work is acknowledged, unfinished work is parked, and tomorrow receives a clear re-entry point.

## An evening reset for the attention you want back

An evening reset is not about optimizing every hour outside work. It is about making a clean transition. Once important context has somewhere reliable to wait, your attention has a better chance to move toward family, rest, music, reading, or simply doing nothing productive.

Early users will help determine which prompts reduce mental clutter, how fast a shutdown can remain useful, and when tomorrow's handoff should return.
